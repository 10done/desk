The wait before the first word

A streamed reply is two stretches of time. The long one is usually the silence before any text arrives.

What this measures

Prefill is the wait until the first piece of the answer shows up. On this page that wait includes the prompt, the queue, and the network, because the clock is on the laptop that sent the request, not inside the GPU. Decode is the time from that first piece to the last one. Total latency is the whole trip, from send to the end of the stream.

The scrubber below is the only control. It moves a playhead through one recorded reply. Time to first token, time per streamed piece, and total latency are recomputed from the pieces that have arrived by that moment. Before the first piece, the phase is prefill and the later numbers stay blank.

The recording

This is one run, slowed down when you press play. The numbers stay on the recorded clock.

Harborline, one question

Harborline is a fictional ferry desk. Its runbook says a departure holds when the harbor wind is at or above 28 knots. The request sent only that page and asked, in one sentence, at what wind speed a departure holds.

The sentence in the recording is the whole answer. It names 28 knots, which is the line on the page. The useful split is in the clock: the screen was empty for most of the wait, and the words themselves were the short tail. When a reply feels slow, the first place to look is the time before the first token.
